RAMP/CAST

A ramp-capacity planner for an FBO at Van Nuys. It forecasts how many jets will want to park during Super Bowl week, warns which hours the ramp overflows, and drafts a plan that a human manager approves.

Focus area: Parking. Arrivals forecasting is the engine underneath; fuel and staffing fall out of the same model. Our FBO is fictional (call it Sepulveda Jet Center), and the airport, the airspace and the history are real.

Why this pitch wins

Three insights the other teams will miss

1 · LA already ran this exam

Super Bowl LVI was played at the same stadium in February 2022. That gives us a real, labelled analog week at the same airports. We can train on everything before it, predict it blind, and report real forecast error. Most teams will only have synthetic data.

2 · Parking is the binding constraint

In 2022 the FAA did not ration runways. It ran a parking reservation program through each FBO, Feb 6 to 14. Slots ran out and NBAA told operators to plan drop-and-go. The ramp is where the week is won or lost.

3 · Overflow burns fuel

A jet that can't park drops its passengers and flies away empty, then comes back empty. Those are two deadhead legs per aircraft. Planning the ramp well cuts empty flights, so the sustainability benefit can be measured in gallons and tonnes of CO₂.

Airspace picture · drawn to scale

Why Van Nuys becomes the hub

Hawthorne sits 1.9 nm from SoFi. LAX, Santa Monica and Torrance are also inside the 10 nm core of the 2022 game-day TFR. Van Nuys is 17 nm out, in the outer ring, with an 8,000 ft runway and four major FBOs. It sees roughly 230,000 operations a year and absorbs the overflow.

The four models

Small, explainable and testable

Use gradient boosting and simulation rather than a black box. Judges score accuracy and explanation, and every number here can be traced back to an input.

M1 · BASELINE

What does an ordinary week look like?

Daily arrivals at KVNY from two or more years of OpenSky flights. Fit a seasonal model (Prophet or LightGBM with day-of-week, month and holiday features). Output a P10/P50/P90 band for the same calendar week without a game.

y = f(dow, month, holiday, trend) ± interval
M2 · EVENT UPLIFT

How much extra, and on which days?

Measure the uplift curve, actual ÷ baseline by day relative to the game, from real analogs. Use LVI LA 2022 for the same basin, plus LVII–LX. Scale by host-basin share. Low, medium and high come from the spread across analogs, not from guesses.

uplift(d) for d ∈ [−5, +1]
M3 · RAMP SIMULATOR

When does the ramp overflow?

Sample arrivals by hour, aircraft size and stay type (drop-and-go vs stays through the game). Run 5,000 Monte Carlo weeks. Report P(occupancy > capacity) per hour. This uncertainty is what we explain to the judges.

P(occ_h > cap) per hour
M4 · PLAN OPTIMIZER

What should we do about it?

A small integer program. Choose reservation caps by day and the drop-and-go quota, and assign overflow to the nearest partner airport to minimize empty legs. Fuel orders and crew shifts follow from the movement forecast.

min empty_leg_nm + unmet_demand · penalty

Responsible AI

Human in command

Judge question · What decision should your AI never be allowed to make?

It never decides whether a specific aircraft may land, park or depart, and it never decides which customer gets the last spot.

Those are safety and fairness calls. Movement authority belongs to ATC and the pilot in command. Ramp safety belongs to the line supervisor. Allocating scarce spots belongs to the FBO manager, under rules a person wrote. Our tool forecasts and drafts. It does not see individual passengers, and it never ranks customers by who they are.

Planning only

Outputs are recommendations with an approve, edit or reject step and an audit log. The tool has no write access to any reservation or ATC system.

Privacy by design

We use aggregate counts by aircraft size class only. Tail numbers are hashed, and LADD-blocked aircraft are excluded. We store no passenger names, manifests or VIP identities.

Uncertainty shown

Every forecast carries a P10–P90 band and a short "why". Low, medium and high scenarios come from the real spread across past events.

Assumptions in the open

Capacity, stay length, fuel per departure and CO₂ factor are editable inputs on screen. They are not hidden constants.

Build order

Roles: Data (OpenSky + registry), Model (M1–M3), Optimizer (M4 + fuel and staffing), Frontend (planner UI), Pitch (story, assumptions sheet, responsible-AI statement).

H0–3
Pull OpenSky arrivals and departures for KVNY and 9 basin airports. Cover Feb 2022, plus 8 ordinary Februaries and Januaries. Download the FAA registry and join on ICAO24. Freeze the dataset.
H3–8
Fit M1 baseline. Build the M2 uplift curve from the LVI 2022 week and published analog counts. Train on data before LVI only.
H8–14
Run the blind LVI backtest and record MAPE against the naive rules. Build the M3 Monte Carlo ramp simulator. Sketch the UI.
H14–20
Build the M4 plan: caps, overflow partners, fuel orders, crew shifts. Wire up the planner UI with the scenario toggle, heatmap and "why" panel. Sleep in shifts.
H20–25
Add the disruption scenario, the CO₂ and empty-leg estimate, and the editable assumptions panel. Write the responsible-AI statement.
H25–30
Freeze code. Rehearse the 3-minute pitch: problem (1 slide), live demo (90 s), backtest proof, the "never" answer. Prepare a backup video of the demo.
Stretch goals

After the core works

LA28 Olympics

Same engine with a different event clock. LA28 runs about 17 days (July 14–30, 2028) with many venues instead of one game, so the uplift curve is longer and flatter. Show the selected-period version for opening ceremony week.

Ground-handoff module

Forecast hourly passenger-arrival waves at the FBO curb so ground transport companies can stage cars. Counts only, with no VIP identities and no routing of named people.

Basin view for regulators

Aggregate all-airport demand as a planning input for airport authorities. It still does not allocate slots, because the FAA and airports do that.
