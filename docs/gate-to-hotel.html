RAMPCAST research · part 2 Super Bowl LXI week · Feb 9–15 2027 AI assists · people decide

GATE → HOTEL

Hundreds of jets leave airports around the world. They all want to land in LA, park, refuel and get their passengers to a hotel. This page maps every step, who controls it, what breaks, and what our system does about it.

Start with the truth

Nobody runs this whole chain today

The journey passes through six different organizations, and each has its own system. Most problems happen at the handoffs between them, which is the gap we fill. Some decisions in the chain belong to people by law, and our system stays out of those.

Landing order is ATC's job

SoCal TRACON and the tower sequence arrivals using time-based metering (FAA TBFM) and wake-turbulence spacing. A Ground Delay Program holds flights at their origin with an EDCT (assigned departure time). No outside tool gets a vote, and none should.

Diverting is the pilot's call

The pilot in command and the operator's dispatcher decide where to go, based on fuel they know and we don't. We can hand them a ranked list of options, but we never make the choice.

The ground side is where nobody coordinates

When a jet diverts from Van Nuys to Burbank, nothing automatically tells the parking plan, the customs office, the chauffeur or the hotel. That is the gap our system fills.

Commercial and private split at the gate

Scheduled airline flights were exempt from the 2026 Super Bowl reservation program and the stadium TFR. Private jets need a reservation (PPR) through an FBO, even for drop-and-go. We model airlines as background traffic and serve the private side.

Worked scenario · fictional flights, real rules

Friday 13:05: Van Nuys loses its long runway

This is the demo moment. One disruption ripples through every lane, and the system re-plans the parts it owns. Every decision that belongs to someone else lands on that person's screen as a recommendation. Flights, times and delays are made up for the demo. The airport layout and the customs hours are real.

InboundSize classFromETACustomsRipple flag at P50 / P90 reopening
13:05

A disabled aircraft closes runway 16R/34L

VNY's 8,000 ft runway is the only one built for large jets, and the parallel runway is about 4,000 ft. Tower closes 16R. The twin estimates reopening at P50 13:55, P90 14:30, based on an assumed history of similar closures. Eight jets inbound to our FBO arrive within the hour.

13:06

The twin re-runs every lane

  • ETAs: each inbound gets a delayed-arrival band. ATC still decides who lands when.
  • Customs: two international arrivals now risk missing VNY customs, which closes 14:30 on Fridays (per CBP's June 2024 sheet).
  • Ramp: holds 8 reserved spots until 15:00, then releases them to the overflow waitlist.
  • Cars: 8 drivers are waiting planeside, so it drafts new staging moves.
13:08

Options go to the operators' dispatchers, not to the cockpit or ATC

For each flight it sends a ranked card: keep VNY with an expected delay, BUR (about 10 nm east) or an airport with customs. Each option shows its runway, open parking at partner FBOs, customs availability and the extra drive time to the booked hotel. The operator weighs fuel, the twin's options and the pilot's judgment, then decides.

13:10

Humans approve

  • FBO GM approves the 90-minute spot hold.
  • Customer service calls CBP's landing-rights line to ask for later inspection for JET-04 and JET-06. The twin drafted the request, and a human makes the call.
  • Ground dispatcher approves moving two cars to Burbank staging after those operators choose BUR.
14:00

Recovery

The runway reopens, and the twin re-sequences the expected arrival wave for line crews and fuel trucks. Hotel ETAs update by booking code. It then logs the actual reopening time so the next forecast improves. Saturday's spot plan is re-optimized so nobody gets boxed in.

What to actually build in 30 hours

Go deep on one thing, and show the whole chain

The track asks for one focus area, and judges reward depth. Make Parking excellent with real data and a backtest. Then show the twin rippling through the other lanes in a single scripted disruption. That shows you understand the whole system without spreading the team too thin.
